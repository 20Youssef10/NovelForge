# Character Profile

Worked example.

Name: Wenna Ardray
Role: Salt-ship apprentice, fire-drawer
Age (at present / at key events): 19 / 16 at the sealing
Pronouns: she/her
Voice/language: `style/CHARACTER_VOICE.md`
Canon status: PERMANENT_CANON

## Identity
Backstory: Harbor-bred, apprenticed to a Guild fire-drawer at eleven. Her mother
died in a wreck the Guild recorded as weather.
Governing core belief: that competence owes a debt to the people who need it
Desires: to be the person her master was, without his compromises
Fears: becoming the kind of person who weighs thirty-one lives against nine and
finds the arithmetic clean
Goals: keep the Cormorant; be told she was wrong about the sealing and be able to
prove it
Flaws: treats her own judgment as more reliable than it is; cannot ask for help
without framing it as an instruction
Secrets: saw her master at the seal stones the night the route closed, and said
nothing
Knowledge boundaries: knows the fire-draw is night-bound and sight-limited (C-001);
does not know who ordered the sealing (U-001); suspects but has not confirmed the
master's involvement

## Behaviour
Decision patterns: decides first, informs second; will commit before she has
enough information and treat the commitment as the information
Stress response: goes quiet and precise, and starts counting things
Moral boundaries: will not seal a vessel while people are aboard; will lie to her
master but not to Maro
Contradictions: desperate to be told she was right, and terrified of being right,
because both cost her the same person
Known constraints: can only draw fire within sight of open water and after dark;
her master's debt on her apprenticeship is unresolved

## Voice
Distinctive diction and rhythm: short declaratives when frightened; counts aloud when
thinking; never uses maritime trade terms she has not heard on a working deck
What she never says directly: that she is afraid for Maro
How she changes with audience: clipped with the Guild, unguarded with Maro
Under duress: sentences get longer and more precise, the opposite of most people

## Arc
Initial state: certain the sealing was a mistake, and certain it was someone else's
Governing belief at start: that truth, once known, obliges action
Wound / flaw: the wreck that killed her mother, unexamined
Pressure: the Guild's offer to certify her, which would end the questioning
Key dilemmas: Chapter 6, the choice of which route to save
Key choices and what each cost: she takes the southern route and Maro's ship
Consequences that changed her: she loses the argument that competence is enough
Reversals: the sealing was, in part, correct — the northern route was already dying
Realisation: the debt she owes is to the decision, not to the outcome
Final state: UNRESOLVED (see U-002)

## Simulation notes
Plausible choices under current pressure: she goes to the seal stones before she
reports to the Guild; she lies about having seen her master
Most likely decision and why: takes the southern route, because thirty-one lives
against nine is not a judgement she is able to make and refusing is the only version
of herself she trusts
Strongest alternative: takes the northern route and loses Maro, which is the version
she cannot survive but might choose
Blocked options: appealing to the Guild council, because she no longer trusts it

## Continuity notes
Night-bound fire-drawing (C-001) governs every combat or crisis scene. Her
apprenticeship debt is unresolved and load-bearing for Chapter 8.
