# Tension Engine v2.1

Track what can be lost, what is unknown, what can change, why it matters, and what pressure is currently active. Tension is not intensity; it is a credible possibility of loss.

## Foundations

Tension requires all of:

- **Stakes** — something the characters would genuinely lose.
- **Uncertainty** — the outcome is not settled in the reader's mind.
- **Pressure** — a force acting now, not merely a condition.
- **Credible consequence** — the danger can actually arrive.

A scene missing any one of these fails differently. No stakes means indifference. No uncertainty means suspense without fear. No pressure means a situation rather than a scene. No credible consequence means announced danger, which reads as noise.

## Kinds

Distinguish where useful:

- **Anticipation** — something is coming.
- **Suspense** — uncertainty about what.
- **Dread** — certainty about a bad outcome.
- **Curiosity** — desire to know.
- **Conflict pressure** — opposing aims in direct contact.
- **Urgency** — a shrinking window.

Different kinds need different instruments. Suspense dies if the outcome is revealed early; dread dies if the outcome is uncertain. Using one where the other is needed is a common failure.

## Record

Maintain audits in `tension/TENSION.md`, including the four-foundations check. A scene missing a foundation fails differently from a scene with weak tension, and the record should say which.

## Detection

- **Fake tension** — danger repeatedly threatened with no believable consequence; the threat has been made so often it cannot land.
- **Empty threats** — a character announces consequences the story never delivers.
- **Outcome certainty** — the reader knows who wins well before the scene does.
- **Manufactured danger** — threats invented to raise intensity rather than arising from the situation.
- **Inverted stakes** — the story treats a real loss as unimportant and an inconvenience as dire.
- **Pressure without agency** — characters are buffeted by events and cannot act.

## Build

Raise tension by narrowing options, shortening time, removing information, increasing cost, or making the outcome depend on a decision — not by increasing volume or adding adjectives. The most reliable escalation is loss of options: what the character could do at the start is no longer available by the end.

## Coordination

Use `pacing-engine` for tempo, `consequence-engine` to make stakes real, `reader-knowledge` for information gaps, `mystery-engine` for withheld outcomes, and `battle-choreography` and `tension-engine` together for action sequences.
